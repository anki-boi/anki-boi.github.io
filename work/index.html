Telehealth clinic · contract · 2026In pilot

A Zoho order desk that checks every order before it moves.

Problem
Every order task in the CRM meant opening the record, confirming payment and paperwork, looking up allergies, working out which pharmacy can ship to that state, and only then keying the order into a portal.
Fix
A local desk reads the task queue from Zoho CRM and turns each task into one approval card: the payment check, the allergies and the record they came from, the pharmacy and the rule that picked it, and the exact note and follow-up it will write. Nothing is written until a person approves, and the portal order is filled in but never submitted by the desk.
Status
It is in pilot with one team, running on that team's orders while it is hardened before wider use.

Live build · e-prescribing portal

From a sheet row to a ready order

Each order used to mean retyping the same patient details into a slow portal. Now the operator pastes the sheet rows and the script does the clicking, then hands control back at every step where judgment matters.

  • A record opens only when ID, phone and full name all agree. A surname alone once pulled up the wrong patient, so anything less now goes to a person.
  • Consent is read three times in a row before it counts, and a restricted shipping route pauses the row.
  • Product names are fuzzy-matched against the live catalog. When there is no clear winner, a person picks and the pick is remembered.
  • It never finishes the sale by itself. The questionnaire is filled for review and the final button is always a person's.

Live build · clinic CRM

Which pharmacy can legally ship here?

Every compounding pharmacy has its own list of states it cannot ship to, and some rules apply to one product only. The pharmacist's mental checklist became a table the screen checks on its own.

  • Shipping rules cover 39 states and territories, across 21 product families.
  • The same product can route differently as an injectable and as a pill, so the form is read from the plan line itself.
  • The worst result wins for the row, and a form the product does not come in reads as unknown instead of borrowing a route.
  • It advises and never decides. A restricted route pauses the order until a person confirms.

Live build · pharmacy ordering

The prescription form, filled and locked

One compounding pharmacy still takes orders as a PDF form. A person picks the products and the length of treatment; the extension does the arithmetic and returns the form filled, locked and ready to review.

  • The person makes the clinical choices and the tool does the arithmetic, the same way every time.
  • It will not produce an incomplete prescription, and it refuses an order that will not fit the form.
  • 29 fields across 4 prescription rows, auto-shrunk to fit, with a field table to check before it is sent.
  • The command-line and browser versions share one engine, and a parity test fails if they ever disagree.

Live build · job hunting

Listing in, scored before you open it

A local dashboard for OnlineJobs.ph, built while I was the one doing the job hunt. Every few hours it checks the board, adds only what is new and pings the desktop when something relevant lands or a tracked job closes.

  • It is careful with the site: one request a second, backing off when told to, and stopping itself when rate-limited.
  • Pay is converted to pesos a month at live exchange rates, and a job with a disliked keyword stays visible if the pay clears your goal.
  • The résumé score is a fixed, explainable rubric. Where AI does tailor a résumé, a checker throws out anything it made up.
  • A parse watchdog turns a silent zero into an alert, so a broken page never passes for "no new jobs today".
