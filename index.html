★ If you came from OnlineJobs.ph

OJ.ph Cleaner

Yes — this is how your listing got read. I built the filter the job board doesn't ship to solve my own hunt, and it is still what I screen with.

Problem
  • The board hands you a list and no filter — pay, freshness and the kind of work aren’t fields: they’re free text on the card, or buried in the description you have to open
  • Salary is incomparable — 5.5$/hr, Php 1000/day, 15-20 AUD per hour and TBD sit side by side
  • Staleness is invisible until you’re pages deep: a 297-job search ran 40–46 days old
  • The board remembers nothing — every screening judgement gets paid for again tomorrow
  • Screening effort scales with how much the market posted, not with how good your match is
Fix
  • A filter the board doesn’t ship: a recency window first, then a salary digit check, then your keywords
  • Negative keywords hide; positive keywords highlight and never hide — a job with a hated keyword that still pays well shows in yellow to reconsider
  • Keywords are exact words or phrases, not substrings — AI matches AI tools, never email
  • Salaries converted to comparable figures at live ECB rates — never guessed
  • Scan opens each listing and re-decides it against its full description — two at a time, capped, cached for 7 days so changing a keyword costs no requests
  • One page appended per scroll — eight Next clicks, gone
Impact
  • Screening effort stops scaling with market volume
  • Every mark on the page names its reason — a filter you can’t audit is one you stop trusting
  • An earlier version demoted off-platform listings and moved 176 of 227 out of High yield — a filter that had stopped filtering. Now pay decides, and a keyword only decorates
  • It refuses to guess: a part-time hourly rate with no stated hours keeps its hourly figure, and the 40 h/week assumption is printed on the card

★ Flagship · In production

Clinical automation suite — 45+ scripts & extensions

Tampermonkey userscripts automating the back office of a live US telehealth clinic: order entry, pharmacy-portal sessions, carrier tracking, patient-data handoffs, SMS/PDF workflows. 40 ship in the public mirror — the rest carry client identifiers, so they stay private.

Problem
  • One prescription meant re-typing the same patient data into whichever pharmacy portal the order needed
  • Then finding its tracking number by hand and pasting it into a sheet
  • Then writing the patient SMS from scratch
  • ~15–20 manual actions per order — each one a chance to pick the wrong patient or the wrong vial
Fix
  • 40 published scripts that each do one job
  • Deliberately not one extension — these portals change their DOM constantly, so isolation keeps a selector fix for FedEx from breaking patient messaging
  • A shared token-based design system gives 40 injected panels one coherent look without a monolith
Impact
  • ~6 min → ~40 s per order, roughly 40–60 hours a month returned
  • Wrong-patient and wrong-vial entries designed out
  • A lookup returning two matches stops for a human; the questionnaire is never auto-submitted
  • Every check the gate runs is a failure that once cost real time

Job Hunter Dashboard

A local job-tracking dashboard for OnlineJobs.ph — built while I was the one doing the job hunt.

Problem
  • Thousands of listings, and no way to tell which ones actually match your résumé
  • Salaries quoted in mixed currencies, converted in your head
  • Applications tracked in a spreadsheet that goes stale exactly when you're busiest
  • No signal on whether a listing has already closed
Fix
  • Live SSE scrape console with keyword / category / skill scoping
  • An ATS score for every listing — deterministic, not LLM, because a rule scorer can't hallucinate a match
  • One-click résumé tailoring that rewrites and reorders only, never invents facts, and falls back rather than shipping bad output
  • A 1-page CV builder that iterates until it really is one page
Impact
  • A multi-hour manual sweep becomes one scoped, pre-scored run
  • Auto-run plus desktop alerts — the tracker tells you when to act instead of going stale
  • A parse watchdog raises a structure-change alert, so a silent zero can't masquerade as “no new jobs today”
  • Spec-driven: CI, pre-push gates and a 359-test suite

True-Anki-MCQ Note Template

An Anki card template built on one uncomfortable observation about how pharmacy students actually study.

Problem
  • Anki's default is pure active recall — retrieving an answer from nothing
  • That's the gold standard for retention, and the right tool when you must produce an answer
  • But the pharmacy board exam is 100% multiple choice — it measures recognition, not production
  • So drilling full recall for a recognition test spends the same study minute on a strictly harder task
Fix
  • Commit to a choice before anything is revealed
  • Choice order randomised on every rep, so position carries no signal
  • Single and multi-answer, with each choice marked correct, wrong or missed — two different study signals
  • A dynamic selection limit that says how many answers exist without revealing which
Impact
  • Familiarity with the real question format, deliberately matched to the target
  • Higher yield per study minute than picture-perfect recall
  • My most-starred repo — a lot of people were quietly over-studying for recognition exams

Anki MCQ Importer

The card-making step, deleted. Semester after semester.

Problem
  • Every exam block arrived as folders of hundreds of lecture-slide images
  • Each card meant reading the slide, deciding what was testable, writing the question, inventing plausible distractors, and typing it in
  • ~1 minute per card — a 500-image block is 8+ hours of transcription before you learn anything
  • So decks got built for the subjects you had time for, usually not the ones you were worst at
Fix
  • Batch-processes folders of images through the Gemini API into structured MCQ cards
  • Context carried across consecutive pages, so a table split over two slides stays coherent
  • Automatic subdeck creation from the parsed subtopic
  • Prompts persist in runtime config — the prompt you spent an evening tuning survives every update
Impact
  • Card authoring goes from ~1 minute each to a batch job
  • Deck size stops being capped by typing stamina
  • An add-on that's safe to update keeps getting fixes

DateCard

A QR-based dating card for the PH market. No app, no algorithm.

Problem
  • There's no good way to show someone who you are when you meet them in public
  • “Find me on Instagram” is an all-or-nothing broadcast — they get the last three years, not the person you just met
  • There's no way to hand over one side of yourself
  • Plenty of people have no public social presence and won't build one just to be findable
  • Dating apps solve discovery for strangers — not for someone you already met
Fix
  • Scan the QR → read the profile → apply to connect → you approve or decline
  • Only then do they get your socials
  • One account holds up to three profiles — serious / casual / friendship — each with its own card and inbox
  • Socials are never in the public page payload: reachable only through an RPC that checks for an accepted application
Impact
  • Replaces “find me on Instagram” with an exchange you control
  • Works in public, no app installed, no algorithm in between
  • You choose who sees which side of you
  • Giving someone a card is more professional than adding a stranger

PH Pharmacy Setup Guide

Everything I know about standing up a community pharmacy in the Philippines, ordered so you can't get lost in it.

Problem
  • Licensing, layout, capital and compliance steps with no published order to them
  • You can't tell which phase you're in — a week on layout drawings while a permit you needed first sits unstarted
  • “Done” is undefined: every step is some paperwork, so there's no moment you can stop thinking about it
  • Progress is invisible for months — the failure that actually ends most setups
Fix
  • Seven ordered phases, 24 entries, worked in order
  • Every entry ticks off, and the tick is remembered
  • A “licensed RPh” toggle hides the pre-license phase
  • Progress stays local: one file, no build, no account
Impact
  • “Where do I start / what's next?” becomes a glance instead of research
  • A months-long goal gets a visible progress loop — deliberately gamified
  • Prints as a document for a partner, landlord or bank
